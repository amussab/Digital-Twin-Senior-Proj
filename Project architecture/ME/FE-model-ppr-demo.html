Bearing stiffness from shaft displacement

Finite-element digital twin of the Bently Nevada RK4 rotor with one 810 g disk at mid-span. The model is given only the two probe readings and has to find the bearing stiffness K. Specification 2: within 10% of a known reference stiffness.

Formulation

Method
Finite element method (FEM), 1D beam model of the shaft
Element type
Euler–Bernoulli beam elements (bending only, shear ignored)
Mesh
20 elements, 21 nodes, 2 degrees of freedom per node (deflection and slope), 42 in total
Shaft
Steel, E = 200 GPa, ρ = 7850 kg/m³, diameter 10 mm, bearing span 400 mm
Bearings
Linear springs of stiffness K at both ends of the span (same K for both)
Disk
Point mass of 810 g at the middle node
Load
Unbalance force at the disk: F = m·e·ω²
Equation solved
[K − ω²M]·x = F (stiffness minus mass effect at running speed, times displacement, equals force)
Identification
Try 1,200 values of K, predict the probe readings for each, keep the K with the smallest mismatch
Check
Rigid bearings + 10 N at mid-span reproduces the textbook deflection PL³/48EI

Assumptions

  1. Bearings are linear springs, no damping.
  2. The unbalance (test mass × radius) is known.
  3. Symmetric rotor, so one K for both bearings.
  4. Probe readings are the steady once-per-revolution (1×) response, with phase from the Keyphasor.
  5. Shaft overhang beyond the bearings and the motor coupling are left out.
  6. Probes are calibrated on the shaft (datasheet scale factor 7.87 V/mm, unit-to-unit error +12.5% / −20%).
  7. Reference bearing stiffness K = 10 MN/m, from a Hertz contact calculation for a 6200 carrying 5.5 N (range 10–15 MN/m). A calculation, not a measurement; see hand calculation B.
  8. Probe noise 0.2 µm per window. Conservative: published eddy-current sensors resolve 0.065–3 µm (Nabavi & Nihtianov, IEEE J. Solid-State Circuits, 2013; Sensors (MDPI), 2022), and even 3 µm gives only 0.03 µm per window; see hand calculation C.

Test setup

How the model finds K

It tries 1,200 stiffness values. For each one it predicts the probe readings, compares them with the measurement, and keeps the value with the smallest mismatch.

B6. At this light load with normal clearance, the bottom ball carries the load

δ=(WKn)2/3=(5.497.21×109)2/3=0.833 μm\delta = \left(\frac{W}{K_n}\right)^{2/3} = \left(\frac{5.49}{7.21\times10^{9}}\right)^{2/3} = 0.833\ \mu\text{m}
K=dQdδ=32Knδ1/2=1.5 Wδ=1.5(5.49)0.833×10−6=9.9×106 N/m≈10 MN/mK = \frac{dQ}{d\delta} = \tfrac{3}{2}K_n\delta^{1/2} = \frac{1.5\,W}{\delta} = \frac{1.5(5.49)}{0.833\times10^{-6}} = 9.9\times10^{6}\ \text{N/m} \approx 10\ \text{MN/m}

With zero clearance, several balls share the load and the same method gives about 15 MN/m. So K = 10–15 MN/m. This is a calculation, not a measurement, and it leaves out the housing's own flexibility.

C. Probe noise

Worst published eddy-current sensor resolution: 3 µm (Sensors, MDPI, 2022). Samples in one 20-revolution window at 1750 rpm and 25 kS/s:

N=fs twindow=25,000×20×601750=17,143N = f_s\,t_{window} = 25{,}000 \times \frac{20 \times 60}{1750} = 17{,}143
σ1×=σraw2N=3 μm×217,143=0.032 μm\sigma_{1\times} = \sigma_{raw}\sqrt{\frac{2}{N}} = 3\ \mu\text{m}\times\sqrt{\frac{2}{17{,}143}} = 0.032\ \mu\text{m}

The demo uses 0.2 µm per window, about 6 times larger, then averages 50 windows and 2 probes:

σavg=0.2 μm50×2=0.020 μm\sigma_{avg} = \frac{0.2\ \mu\text{m}}{\sqrt{50 \times 2}} = 0.020\ \mu\text{m}
